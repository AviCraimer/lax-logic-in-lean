\section{Introduction}\label{1-introduction}

The object of this paper is a reading of the modality \ensuremath{\bigcirc}
characterised by the axiom schemes

\begin{lstlisting}
  ◯R : M ⊃ ◯M
  ◯M : ◯◯M ⊃ ◯M
  ◯S : (◯M ∧ ◯N) ⊃ ◯(M ∧ N)
\end{lstlisting}

with Modus Ponens and the rule ``from \ensuremath{M \supset N} infer \ensuremath{\bigcirc M \supset \bigcirc N}''. The
introduction of \cite{FairtloughMendler1997} motivated \ensuremath{\bigcirc} by a list of six perspectives ---
Curry\textquotesingle s 1948 lectures~\cite{Curry1950,Curry1952}, nuclei on complete
Heyting algebras~\cite{Macnab1981,Johnstone1982,PicadoPultr2012}, Kripke
and J-space semantics~\cite{Kripke1965,Goldblatt1981,Simpson1994}, the
strong monads of computational type
theory~\cite{Moggi1991,BentonBiermanDePaiva1998,Kobayashi1997,
PfenningDavies2001},
the proof-theoretic constraint
reading~\cite{FairtloughMendlerWalton1997}, and the timing analysis of
combinational circuits~\cite{Mendler2000} --- and settled on the constraint reading: \ensuremath{\bigcirc M} as
``for some constraint c, formula M holds under c''. This paper adds a
seventh reading and pursues it:

(7) \ensuremath{\bigcirc M} is ``\textbf{M is believed}''. The unit \ensuremath{\bigcirc R} says ``what is true is
believed''; the multiplication \ensuremath{\bigcirc M} says ``what is believed to be believed
is believed''; \ensuremath{\bigcirc S} says ``beliefs combine''. The reading is doxastic and
not epistemic: nothing gives \ensuremath{\bigcirc M \supset} M, and indeed \ensuremath{\bigcirc\mathit{false}} is consistent ---
a believer may hold an absurd belief without thereby believing
everything. An inconsistent belief does not entail every formula.

Two features distinguish the present pursuit of (7) from a routine
exercise in modal-logic reinterpretation, and they are connected.

The first is that the reading is justified by \emph{theorems}, the central
ones new. Classically the reading is degenerate: every nucleus on a
Boolean algebra is closed, so a classical \ensuremath{\bigcirc} has the single form
\ensuremath{\bigcirc M \equiv M \vee \bigcirc\mathit{false}}, fixed by the one
proposition \ensuremath{\bigcirc\mathit{false}}, its ``credulity level''. The total
sceptic (\ensuremath{\bigcirc\mathit{false} \equiv \mathit{false}}, so
\ensuremath{\bigcirc M \equiv M}) and the totally credulous believer
(\ensuremath{\bigcirc\mathit{false} \equiv \mathit{true}}, so
\ensuremath{\bigcirc M \equiv \mathit{true}}) are its extremes; every classical
believer has this same form, differing only in the disjoined proposition, so
there is no structural variety among them. A genuine spectrum of distinct
believers appears only over intuitionistic logic ---
open nuclei, invisible classically, model belief under a hypothesis,
and the closed \ensuremath{\bigcirc}-fragment of even one free algebra is infinite --- and,
by the context-completeness theorem descending from Curry\textquotesingle s problem, a
proof of \ensuremath{\bigcirc M} contains the evidence for believing M. Belief
backed by such evidence is \emph{evidential} belief, and evidential belief is
non-degenerate only over intuitionistic logic. Making this argument
precise occupies Sections 3--6, whose main novelty is a realisability
semantics in which this claim becomes a completeness theorem: PLL is
exactly the logic of belief whose evidence may react to the future of
the inquiry.

The second feature is methodological. Every claim in this paper is
machine-checked, in a Lean 4 formalisation that also contains the whole
of \cite{FairtloughMendler1997} --- the calculi and their equivalences, cut elimination,
the constraint semantics, soundness, completeness, decidability --- and
the axiomatic footprint of every theorem is tracked, most of the new
material being choice-free. We do not regard the formalisation as an
audit performed after the mathematics. Three times in this development
the attempt to prove a plausible statement produced instead a proof of
its negation, and each failure redirected the theory; the most
consequential of these, the obstruction of Section 5, is the reason the
completeness-grade semantics has the shape it has. In our experience the
proof assistant reliably exposes cases we had overlooked. Section 9
reports the method, including the
division of labour between the human author and the language model that
operated the proof assistant.

The paper is organised as follows. Section 2 fixes the mechanised base
and recalls the constraint semantics. Section 3 proves the classical
collapse and the constructive richness. Section 4 defines evidence and
the three realisability clause families; Section 5 proves the
separation theorems and the obstruction; Section 6 the completeness
theorem, in three steps --- decoration, the finitised canonical model,
and their composition. Section 7 runs the theory: executable
countermodels read as belief states, and a proof compiled to its
evidence. Sections 8--10 treat related work, method, and open problems.

\section{The mechanised base}\label{2-the-mechanised-base}

The formulas of PLL are generated by the grammar

\begin{lstlisting}
M ::= A | false | M ∧ M | M ∨ M | M ⊃ M | ◯M
\end{lstlisting}

over a countable supply of propositional constants; \ensuremath{\lnot}M abbreviates
M \ensuremath{\supset} false. The artefact presents PLL as a natural deduction system with
membership-based contexts (\texttt{LaxND}, \texttt{PLLNDCore.lean}), as an
intrinsically-typed term calculus in the sense of the computational
lambda-calculus (\texttt{Tm}, \texttt{PLLTerms.lean}), as a Hilbert system, and as a
cut-free single-succedent sequent calculus (\texttt{SC}, \texttt{PLLSequent.lean});
all four are proved equivalent; cut elimination, the disjunction
property, \ensuremath{\bigcirc}-reflection and conservativity over intuitionistic
propositional logic (\texttt{conservativity\_IPL}) are theorems, and the term calculus is
strongly normalising for \ensuremath{\beta} together with the monadic associativity law,
by a \ensuremath{\top\top}-lifting argument~\cite{LindleyStark2005} that appears to be the first mechanised one
with sums (\texttt{PLLTopTop.lean}). Decidability is likewise mechanised, as a
terminating, kernel-honest procedure over a contraction-free
calculus~\cite{Dyckhoff1992,Iemhoff2024} ---
with the wrinkle, documented in the artefact and communicated
separately to the calculus\textquotesingle s author, that the published G4-style calculus for PLL
is provably incomplete and a small repair is both necessary and
sufficient. Craig interpolation holds, by a Maehara induction over the
cut-free calculus, stated choice-free through an explicitly
constructive finite-set toolkit (\texttt{craig\_interpolation'},
Appendix~\ref{app:base}); uniform interpolation, by contrast, remains
open (Section 10). After an axiom-hygiene pass, the entire
proof-theoretic chain --- interpolation included --- audits at
\texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}: no choice.

The semantics is that of \cite{FairtloughMendler1997}, Definitions 3.1--3.2, mechanised
verbatim (\texttt{PLLKripke.lean}):

\begin{lstlisting}
structure ConstraintModel where
  W : Type
  Ri : W → W → Prop   -- information order
  Rm : W → W → Prop   -- constraint order; Rm ⊆ Ri
  F : Set W           -- fallible worlds
  V : String → Set W  -- valuation; full on F
  ...                 -- eight structural conditions
\end{lstlisting}

Forcing is as in intuitionistic logic on the frame (W, \ensuremath{R_{i}}, V), with
false holding exactly at the fallible worlds, and the modal clause is
the \ensuremath{\forall\exists} statement

\begin{lstlisting}
w ⊨ ◯N  iff  for all v with w Rᵢ v
             there exists u with v Rₘ u, u ⊨ N
\end{lstlisting}

The clause endows \ensuremath{\bigcirc} with properties both of possibility and of
necessity, as the original paper observed. Under reading (7) it is a
stability statement. Call N \emph{credible} at a world v when a
constraint step from v reaches a world satisfying N:

\begin{lstlisting}
N is credible at v  iff  ∃ u. v Rₘ u and u ⊨ N
\end{lstlisting}

Then w \ensuremath{\vDash \bigcirc N} says exactly that N \emph{remains credible in all
future worlds}: however inquiry develops, some admissible discharge of
the constraint still verifies N. The
fallible worlds --- worlds with inconsistent information, validating
everything --- are what keep \ensuremath{\bigcirc\mathit{false}} satisfiable, and under (7) they are
the semantic counterpart of an inconsistently held belief. Soundness for sequents is
\texttt{soundness} (\texttt{PLLKripke.lean}, audit \texttt{[propext]}); the classical
completeness theorem of \cite[\S4]{FairtloughMendler1997} is \texttt{completeness}
(\texttt{PLLCompleteness.lean}), kept in the artefact deliberately, Zorn's
lemma included, as the foil for Section 6.2.

\section{Classical belief collapses; constructive belief is rich}\label{3-classical-belief-collapses-constructive-belief-is-rich}

We first make precise the claim that reading (7) has no classical
content.

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{nucleus\_eq\_sup\_bot}, \texttt{nucleusOrderIsoBot}]\label{thm:collapse}
On a
Boolean algebra every nucleus is closed: j x = \ensuremath{x \sqcup j \bot}. The nuclei on B
form a lattice order-isomorphic to B itself.
\end{theorem}

Under reading (7), a classical believer is determined by one proposition,
a = \ensuremath{\bigcirc\mathit{false}}, the strongest thing believed for no reason; belief is then
``M, or a'', which adds a to every proposition alike. At
a = false the believer is the total sceptic, believing only what is
true; at a = true, the totally credulous believer. Nothing between these two
distinguishes one classical believer from another except the choice of
a. Classically there is no structure of belief, only a
degree of laxity. (The algebraic fact is
classical~\cite{BeazerMacnab1979,Macnab1981}, with the modern treatment
of~\cite{AvilaBezhanishviliMorandiZaldivar2020}; the mechanisation, the
sharp order-isomorphism, and the reading are what is added here.)

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{em\_of\_openNucleus\_eq\_closedNucleus}, \texttt{closed\_lax\_infinite}]\label{thm:openclosed}
Intuitionistically, the open nucleus \ensuremath{u_{a}} = (a \ensuremath{\supset \cdot}) coincides with a
closed nucleus exactly when excluded middle holds at a; and the closed
\ensuremath{\bigcirc}-fragment RN(\ensuremath{\bigcirc}, \{\}) of the free algebra on no generators is infinite.
\end{theorem}

The open nucleus is belief under the hypothesis a --- the logic
of conditional commitment, ``believing M modulo a'' --- and classically it is
indistinguishable from a closed nucleus, precisely because \ensuremath{a \supset M} and M \ensuremath{\vee \lnot}a coincide there. That the
closed fragment of even the generator-free algebra is infinite says the
constructive believer has infinitely many pairwise inequivalent modal
degrees where the classical believer had a single parameter (the
embedding is through the Rieger--Nishimura
lattice~\cite{Rieger1949,Nishimura1960}; nuclei over such
non-complete Heyting algebras are studied
in~\cite{Erne2022,ErnePicadoPultr2022}).

The smallest algebra already exhibits the separation, and the artefact
enumerates it exactly (\texttt{BeliefExamples.lean}, kernel-checked by
\texttt{decide}): on the three-element chain there are precisely four
believers (\texttt{chain3\_card}) --- the sceptic, the credulous believer, one
dogmatic (closed) and one hypothetical (open) --- and the last two
differ (\texttt{chain3\_open\_ne\_closed}). Hypothetical belief is a new
attitude from the first non-Boolean algebra onwards.

\begin{theorem}[context completeness]\label{thm:ctx}
PLL \ensuremath{\vdash \varphi} iff for every
standard constraint context C, IPL \ensuremath{\vdash \varphi}\^{}C; and no finite set of
constraint contexts suffices.
(\texttt{Ctx.thm6}, \texttt{Ctx.corollary10}.)
\end{theorem}

This — mechanised after the Curry-problem analysis of TYPES
2000~\cite{FairtloughMendler2002} — is the theorem that makes
``evidential'' more than a slogan: a proof
of \ensuremath{\bigcirc M} is, uniformly in the constraint, a proof of M under that
constraint --- the grounds are contained in the belief. Taken together,
Theorems 3.1--3.3 establish the paper\textquotesingle s central argument: classically
belief collapses; constructively it is rich; the richness is
evidential; and so a non-degenerate logic of evidential belief is
possible only over intuitionistic logic.
One more deserves recording here: the standard constraint contexts
themselves form a Boolean algebra (\texttt{Ctx.thm2\_boolean\_algebra})
--- the space of a believer\textquotesingle s possible grounds is classical even
though the belief those grounds discipline is not.

\begin{proposition}[the doxastic profile]\label{prop:profile}
\ensuremath{\bigcirc} is a normal modality (it validates K, and \ensuremath{\bigcirc\top} = \ensuremath{\top}); the derived
rule ``from \ensuremath{\Gamma \vdash} M infer \ensuremath{\bigcirc\Gamma \vdash \bigcirc M}'' holds --- logical omniscience;
introspection is exact, \ensuremath{\bigcirc\bigcirc}M \ensuremath{\dashv\vdash \bigcirc M}; and there is no factivity and
no D axiom: \ensuremath{\nvdash \lnot\bigcirc}false and \ensuremath{\nvdash \bigcirc\mathit{false}}, while
\ensuremath{\bigcirc\mathit{false} \vdash \bigcirc M} for every M.
(\texttt{BeliefIdealisation.lean}, \texttt{BeliefNormality.lean},
\texttt{BeliefFalsum.lean}.)
\end{proposition}

This, following Hintikka~\cite{Hintikka1962}, is the idealisation
adopted openly: the unit says the believer accepts what
is true, and omniscience over consequence is the price of being a
closure operator. The underivabilities are by the countermodels of
\cite[Fig.~3]{FairtloughMendler1997}; the last clause is the credulous
collapse, confined to the \ensuremath{\bigcirc}-level --- a believer of the absurd
believes everything, and nothing is thereby true.

\subsection{The ladder of logics}\label{31-the-ladder-of-logics}

Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} says that classically a believer is one
parameter; Theorem~\ref{thm:openclosed} says that constructively the
closed fragment is infinite. Between those two statements there is a
measurement to be made, and it can be made exactly. Read
RN(\ensuremath{\bigcirc}, \{\}) as the believer\textquotesingle s vocabulary for
describing its own credulity: the propositions expressible from
\ensuremath{\bigcirc} and \ensuremath{\mathit{false}} alone, up to
interderivability --- ``I believe the absurd'', ``I do not believe the
absurd'', ``I do not disbelieve that I believe the absurd'', and so on
upwards. Constructively that vocabulary is infinite. The following
theorem says what each classical principle costs.

\begin{theorem}[the ladder]\label{thm:ladder}
Let A range over the closed formulas --- those built from
\ensuremath{\mathit{false}} and \ensuremath{\bigcirc} with no propositional
constants. Up to interderivability there are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item over PLL, infinitely many classes (\texttt{closed\_lax\_infinite});
\item over PLL + linearity \ensuremath{(A \supset B) \vee (B \supset A)},
  exactly six --- \ensuremath{\bot}, \ensuremath{\lnot\bigcirc\bot},
  \ensuremath{\bigcirc\bot},
  \ensuremath{\bigcirc\bot \vee \lnot\bigcirc\bot},
  \ensuremath{\lnot\lnot\bigcirc\bot}, \ensuremath{\top}
  (\texttt{varfree\_exactly\_six}; pairwise distinct, \texttt{six\_pairwise});
\item over PLL + excluded middle, exactly four --- \ensuremath{\bot},
  \ensuremath{\bigcirc\bot}, \ensuremath{\lnot\bigcirc\bot}, \ensuremath{\top}
  (\texttt{varfree\_exactly\_four}; distinct, \texttt{four\_distinct});
\item over PLL + \ensuremath{\lnot\bigcirc\bot}, exactly two, \ensuremath{\bot}
  and \ensuremath{\top} (\texttt{varfree\_dichotomy}).
\end{enumerate}
Clauses (2) and (3) hold already for the \emph{single closed axioms}
\ensuremath{\lnot\bigcirc\bot \vee \lnot\lnot\bigcirc\bot} and
\ensuremath{\bigcirc\bot \vee \lnot\bigcirc\bot}
(\texttt{varfree\_exactly\_six\_wem0},
\texttt{varfree\_exactly\_four\_em0}), so each count is stable across every
system between that one axiom and the full scheme.
\end{theorem}

The ladder is the quantitative form of the paper\textquotesingle s
argument. The space between the total sceptic and the totally credulous
believer is vast --- infinite already in the fragment that mentions no
subject matter at all --- and it is constructivity alone that keeps it
so. Linearity of the evidential order cuts the infinity to six;
excluded middle cuts it to four; and the further assumption that belief
is consistent, \ensuremath{\lnot\bigcirc\bot}, cuts it to two, at which
point the believer can no longer say anything about its own credulity
that is not already \ensuremath{\bot} or \ensuremath{\top}. The last two
rungs recover the two poles of Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse}: at four
classes the parameter \ensuremath{a = \bigcirc\mathit{false}} still
ranges, and at two it has been fixed at \ensuremath{\bot}. Each rung is
also a completeness theorem: PLL + linearity is sound and complete for
the \emph{connected} constraint models, those whose
\ensuremath{R_{i}}-successor sets are chains
(\texttt{derivLin\_iff\_valid}), by the canonical construction of
Section~\ref{62-the-countermodels-finitely-and-without-zorn} relativised
to prime theories containing the scheme.

\begin{proposition}[the modality on each rung]\label{prop:rungs}
At six classes \ensuremath{\bigcirc\lnot\bigcirc\bot \dashv\vdash \bigcirc\bot \vee \lnot\bigcirc\bot}
(\texttt{box\_nobot}); at four,
\ensuremath{\bigcirc\lnot\bigcirc\bot \dashv\vdash \top}
(\texttt{box\_nobot\_em}), so \ensuremath{\bigcirc} acts there as the closed
nucleus \ensuremath{x \mapsto x \vee \bigcirc\bot}. Under excluded middle
together with \ensuremath{\lnot\bigcirc\bot} the modality is trivial:
\ensuremath{\bigcirc A \dashv\vdash A} for \emph{every} A, closed or not
(\texttt{box\_trivial}).
\end{proposition}

The last clause is a remark of~\cite[p.~6]{FairtloughMendler1997}, here
machine-checked; under reading (7) it is the exact statement that a
classical believer who is guaranteed consistent is the total sceptic and
nothing else. Booleanness is doing the work and not merely the
excluded-middle instance: on a general Heyting algebra the closed form
fails, witnessed by a nucleus on the three-element chain with
\ensuremath{j\bot = \bot} but \ensuremath{j\,1 = 2 \neq 1}
(\texttt{nucleus\_not\_closed\_Fin3}), which is why the classical rung
has four elements and not two.

\begin{proposition}[what forces distribution]\label{prop:dist}
K is a theorem of plain PLL, and K does not force the distribution
scheme \ensuremath{\bigcirc(A \vee B) \supset (\bigcirc A \vee \bigcirc B)}
(\texttt{K\_does\_not\_force\_dist}). Linearity does: each instance follows
from the single instance \ensuremath{(A \supset B) \vee (B \supset A)}
(\texttt{dist\_of\_lin}). The converse of K fails, and fails already on a
\emph{linear} model: \ensuremath{\bigcirc A \supset \bigcirc B \nvdash \bigcirc(A \supset B)},
by two certified countermodels, one infallible and linear, one with a
fallible world (\texttt{converseK\_fails\_infallible},
\texttt{converseK\_fails\_fallible}).
\end{proposition}

So the chain-based validity of distribution is financed by linearity of
the evidential order, not by normality; a believer whose grounds are
linearly ordered must, on being persuaded of a disjunction, be
persuaded of a disjunct, because one of the two implications between
them is already available. That the converse of K fails even there is
the doxastic point that believing B whenever one believes A is weaker
than believing the conditional.

Two structural facts about the fragment complete the picture. First, its
\ensuremath{\bigcirc}-depth stratification is strict. Writing
\ensuremath{D_{0} = \{\bot, \top\}}, \ensuremath{D_{1}} for the ladder
generated by \ensuremath{\bigcirc\bot}, and \ensuremath{D_{n+1}} for the
Heyting algebra generated by
\ensuremath{D_{n} \cup \bigcirc D_{n}}, the class depth of
\ensuremath{\bigcirc g_{1}} is exactly three, so
\ensuremath{D_{2} \subsetneq D_{3}}
(\texttt{depth\_box\_gap\_one\_exact}): nesting the modality genuinely
buys new belief states, and the hierarchy does not collapse at the first
opportunity. Whether the depth-three layer is itself infinite --- that
is, whether the whole \ensuremath{\bigcirc g} antichain sits at class
depth three --- is open. Second, the fragment has identifiable points:
calling a closed A \emph{visible} when \ensuremath{\uparrow}A is proper
and prime, so that A names a point of the Esakia dual, the classes of
\ensuremath{\top}, \ensuremath{t_{1}}, \ensuremath{t_{2}},
\ensuremath{t_{4}} and \ensuremath{t_{6}} are visible, while no odd rung
from \ensuremath{t_{3}} upwards is (\texttt{visible\_top},
\texttt{visible\_rnSub\_\{one,two,four,six\}},
\texttt{not\_joinPrime\_rnSub\_odd}). The engine is a Harrop lemma for
PLL: \ensuremath{\bigcirc A} is Harrop for arbitrary A, because the
succedent of the left rule for \ensuremath{\bigcirc} must itself be
\ensuremath{\bigcirc}-shaped, so a believed hypothesis can never help
produce a disjunction. Under reading (7): what is merely believed never
settles an open question, however much it settles.

\section{Evidence}\label{4-evidence}

We now equip the constraint models with evidence; the ancestry is
Kleene realisability~\cite{Kleene1945,Troelstra1973,Troelstra1998,
TroelstraVanDalen1988} in its Kripke-indexed form~\cite{Lipton1992}
(evidence-function possible-world
models~\cite{Fitting2005,MarinPadhiarShillito2026} are closely
related), with partial applicative
structures the standard carriers~\cite{VanOosten2008}. The equipment
is deliberately light.

\begin{lstlisting}
structure Pca where
  Carrier : Type
  app : Carrier → Carrier → Option Carrier
  pair : Carrier → Carrier → Carrier
  fst snd : Carrier → Carrier
  tag : Bool → Carrier → Carrier
  untag : Carrier → Bool × Carrier
  ...        -- pairing and tagging laws only

structure Evidence
    (P : Pca) (C : ConstraintModel) where
  E : String → C.W → Set P.Carrier
  hered_E : ∀ {s w v}, C.Ri w v → E s w ⊆ E s v
  full_E  : ∀ {s w}, w ∈ C.F → ∀ x, x ∈ E s w
\end{lstlisting}

A partial applicative structure with total pairing and tag decoding ---
no combinatory completeness is assumed at this stage --- and, for each
atom and world, a set of elements evidencing that atom there. Evidence
is hereditary (``belief increases along the branching order''; heredity
of all the realisability relations below is a theorem) and full at
fallible worlds, mirroring the fullness of the valuation there.

Three realisability relations are defined over this equipment. They
agree at atoms (membership in the evidence set, or fallibility), at
false (fallibility), at \ensuremath{\wedge} (pairing) and at \ensuremath{\vee} (tagging), and differ only
at \ensuremath{\supset} and \ensuremath{\bigcirc} --- and there only in \emph{how much the realiser is given about the
future}. Uniform realisability \ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{u}}} gives it nothing:

\begin{lstlisting}
x ⊩ᵘ M ⊃ N at w  iff  for all v ≥ w
                      and all b ⊩ᵘ M at v,
                      x·b is defined, ⊩ᵘ N at v;
x ⊩ᵘ ◯M at w     iff  for all v ≥ w there is u
                      with v Rₘ u, x ⊩ᵘ M at u
\end{lstlisting}

--- the same element must serve every future and every
constraint-witness. Strategy realisability \ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{s}}}, relative to an injective
coding \ensuremath{\kappa} of worlds into the carrier, makes \ensuremath{\bigcirc}-evidence a function of the
presented future:

\begin{lstlisting}
| .somehow φ, x, w =>
    ∀ v, C.Ri w v → v ∈ C.F ∨
      (∃ y, P.app x (κ v) = some y ∧
        ∃ u, C.Rm v u ∧ P.fst y = κ u ∧
          realS P Ev κ φ (P.snd y) u)
\end{lstlisting}

In words: evidence for \ensuremath{\bigcirc M} is a strategy which, handed the code of
whatever future v has actually arrived, answers with a package naming a
constraint-witness u --- naming it, as \ensuremath{\kappa} u, rather than merely having
one --- together with evidence for M there. The strategy has no
foreknowledge; the future is its input. The witness must be named
because the elimination rule\textquotesingle s computational content applies its
continuation at that name; here the semantics tracks proofs, not merely
truth.

Presented-strategy realisability \ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{p}}} does the same at
implication: the realiser of \ensuremath{M \supset N} receives the pair \ensuremath{\langle\kappa} v, b\ensuremath{\rangle} of the
presented world and the argument:

\begin{lstlisting}
| .ifThen φ ψ, x, w =>
    ∀ v, C.Ri w v → v ∈ C.F ∨
      (∀ b, realP P Ev κ φ b v →
        ∃ y, P.app x (P.pair (κ v) b) = some y ∧
          realP P Ev κ ψ y v)
\end{lstlisting}

One might suspect this of being a convenience. Section 5 shows it is
forced.

\section{The separations, and the obstruction}\label{5-the-separations-and-the-obstruction}

The three relations are separated on one three-world model --- the split
model: a root r below two incomparable leaves, A true at one leaf and B
at the other --- together with small variants.

\begin{theorem}[the separations; \texttt{BeliefRealisability.lean}]\label{thm:separations}
(i) (\texttt{bite\_uniform\_split}) At the root of the split
model \ensuremath{\bigcirc}(\ensuremath{A \vee B}) is truth-forced, but no element \ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{u}}}-realises it.
(ii) (\texttt{uniform\_dist\_valid}) An identity element \ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{u}}}-realises
\ensuremath{\bigcirc}(\ensuremath{A \vee B}) \ensuremath{\supset} (\ensuremath{\bigcirc A \vee \bigcirc B}) at every world of every model --- a
scheme PLL does not prove (\texttt{not\_provable\_somehow\_or\_dist}).
(iii) (\texttt{strategy\_realises\_obAB}, \texttt{strategy\_dist\_refuted})
Under \ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{s}}} a case-splitting strategy realises \ensuremath{\bigcirc}(\ensuremath{A \vee B}) at the
root, and the distribution scheme acquires a countermodel.
(iv) (\texttt{impdist\_not\_uniform})
(\ensuremath{\bigcirc A \supset \bigcirc B}) \ensuremath{\supset \bigcirc}(\ensuremath{A \supset B}) is not \ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{u}}}-valid.
\end{theorem}

Read in order. A formula can be forced without being uniformly realised
(i): a uniform realiser carries one tag, and would have to decide the
disjunct before the branch occurs. Rigid evidence validates a strictly
stronger logic (ii), which we call PLL\ensuremath{{}^{\mathrm{u}}};
Section 10 identifies its natural candidate axiomatisation and what
remains open. Strategy evidence supplies a realiser and restores the
countermodel (iii), and is the first point in the development where
evidence is literally a program. Uniformity also fails at implication (iv).

The natural question is then whether \ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{s}}} is the completeness-grade
relation. It is not, and the reason is a theorem.

\begin{theorem}[the obstruction; \texttt{realS\_fullness\_obstruction}, audit
\texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}]\label{thm:obstruction}
There is a three-world constraint model on
which no evidence assignment whatever is simultaneously atom-adequate
(evidence only where the atom holds) and full for \ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{s}}} (every forced
formula has a realiser) --- for every applicative structure able to
implement finite lookup tables against the world-coding.
\end{theorem}

\begin{lstlisting}
theorem realS_fullness_obstruction
    (P : Pca) (κ : obsC.W → P.Carrier)
    (htab : ∀ g : obsC.W → P.Carrier,
      ∃ s, ∀ i, P.app s (κ i) = some (g i)) :
    ¬ ∃ Ev : Evidence P obsC,
      (∀ (a : String) (w : obsC.W),
        ∀ x ∈ Ev.E a w, w ∈ obsC.V a) ∧
      (∀ (φ : PLLFormula) (w : obsC.W),
        obsC.force w φ → ∃ x, x ⊩ˢ[Ev, κ, w] φ)
\end{lstlisting}

Note the quantifier structure: the evidence assignment is inside the
negation. This is an obstruction, not a counterexample to one
decoration. Since adequacy together with fullness is exactly the mutual
induction any completeness-by-decoration proof must run --- each consumes
the other at the \ensuremath{\supset}-clause --- completeness of PLL for \ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{s}}} is not provable
this way on these frames.

\begin{proof}
The model has a root below two incomparable leaves; an atom t holds at
both leaves, p only at the first, q only at the second; the constraint
relation is trivial. The proof is short, and we give it in full. Fullness
at the atom t provides tokens \ensuremath{m_{1}}, \ensuremath{m_{2}} at the two leaves. One
application of the table hypothesis then produces a \emph{single} element s
realising \ensuremath{\bigcirc t} at both leaves simultaneously: presented \ensuremath{\kappa}(leaf), it
answers with that leaf\textquotesingle s own name and that leaf\textquotesingle s token; and nothing
about s records which leaf it is applied to, since a strategy is a function
of the presented future and carries no world-marks. Now \ensuremath{\bigcirc t \supset} (\ensuremath{p \vee q}) is
truth-forced at the root (vacuously there, since \ensuremath{\bigcirc t} fails at the root;
via the local atom at each leaf), so fullness provides a realiser x.
The \ensuremath{\supset}-clause of \ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{s}}} gives x the argument alone; instantiating it at each
leaf with the same argument s, application being a function, both
instantiations receive the same answer y --- whose tag must be false at
the p-leaf, since atom-adequacy has emptied the q-evidence there, and
true at the q-leaf for the symmetric reason. This is a contradiction: a
Boolean value cannot be both true and false.
\end{proof}

Two remarks. First, the obstruction is \ensuremath{\bigcirc}-essential. For a purely
intuitionistic antecedent the argument collapses: world-tagged atom
evidence would let a table-building structure case on the argument
after all, and heredity ensures that forcing a disjunction at a branch
point has already committed to a disjunct. What cannot carry
world-marks is precisely a strategy, and that is the source of the
obstruction to naive completeness.
Second, the repair is thereby forced, not chosen: the \ensuremath{\supset}-clause
must be given the world, exactly as the \ensuremath{\bigcirc}-clause already is, and that
single change is the definition of \ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{p}}}. The separations survive the
repair untouched, being stated for \ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{u}}} and \ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{s}}}. This is the section\textquotesingle s conclusion,
and the paper\textquotesingle s central claim: \textbf{what one may assume about how evidence
meets the future determines which logic of belief one obtains.}

\section{Completeness}\label{6-completeness}

In this section we prove completeness of PLL with respect to
\ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{p}}}-realisability. The proof has two independent halves --- a decoration
theorem, saying that every suitable countermodel refutes in the
realisability sense, and a countermodel-existence theorem --- and we take
them in that order. Both halves are effective in a sense made precise
below; the existence half finitises the canonical construction of
\cite[\S4]{FairtloughMendler1997}, replacing its one appeal to
Zorn\textquotesingle s lemma~\cite{Zorn1935} with an iterated appeal to the
mechanised decision procedure.

\subsection{Decoration: adequacy and fullness}\label{61-decoration-adequacy-and-fullness}

The frames to be decorated are \emph{finite models as data}: the structure
\texttt{FinCM} (\texttt{PLLCountermodelEmit.lean}) packages worlds 0,\ldots,n\ensuremath{-}1, the two
relations as lists of pairs, the fallible set and the valuation as
lists, together with an executable forcing function \texttt{forceB} and a
verified reflection lemma (\texttt{force\_iff}) identifying the computation
with genuine forcing in the induced constraint model. On such a frame,
\emph{token evidence} gives each atom one designated element, exactly where
the atom holds. All the evidence a completeness proof needs, it turns
out, is one token per atom.

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{realP\_adequate\_and\_full}, audit
\texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}]\label{thm:adequacy-fullness}
Over any checked finite frame with token
evidence, and any applicative structure with two table-forming
capacities against the world-coding: for every formula \ensuremath{\varphi},

\begin{lstlisting}
(∀ w x,
   (x ⊩ᵖ[tokenEvidence P M h tok, κ, w] φ) →
   (M.toModel h).force w φ) ∧
(∃ wit : Fin M.n → P.Carrier, ∀ w,
   (M.toModel h).force w φ →
   (wit w) ⊩ᵖ[tokenEvidence P M h tok, κ, w] φ)
\end{lstlisting}

--- whatever is realised is forced (adequacy), and whatever is forced is
realised by an explicit per-world witness function (fullness).
\end{theorem}

The shape of the fullness clause deserves a remark before the proof:
not ``at each world there is a witness'' but ``there is a function of
worlds''. The induction requires the whole function at once, because the
witnesses at \ensuremath{\supset} and \ensuremath{\bigcirc} are tables whose entries are the subformula\textquotesingle s
witnesses at other worlds.

\begin{proof}
By a single induction on \ensuremath{\varphi} proving the conjunction; each
case may use both halves of the inductive hypotheses, and the cases at
\ensuremath{\supset} exhibit the mutual dependency in both directions. Throughout, the
reflection lemma converts freely between forcing and its executable
form, and this is what makes the theorem constructive: every
branch decision in a witness is a computation, not a case split on an
undecided proposition.

At atoms, false, and \ensuremath{\wedge} there is nothing surprising. At \ensuremath{\vee},
adequacy reads the realiser\textquotesingle s tag, and the fullness witness
\emph{computes} the branch: it runs the executable forcing of the left
disjunct at w and tags accordingly --- no excluded middle is consulted;
the decision procedure for forcing is. At \ensuremath{\bigcirc}, adequacy is a direct
reading of the strategy\textquotesingle s answers, and fullness tabulates a bounded,
decidable search of the finite frame for a constraint-successor forcing
the body, which the \ensuremath{\forall\exists} clause guarantees to succeed. (Both
witness computations are given in full in
Appendix~\ref{app:canonical}.)

At \ensuremath{\supset}, the case where the obstruction is answered. Adequacy: given a
realiser x of \ensuremath{\varphi \supset \psi} and a future v forcing \ensuremath{\varphi},
feed x the pair \ensuremath{\langle\kappa} v, wit\ensuremath{\varphi} v\ensuremath{\rangle} --- the antecedent\textquotesingle s fullness witness --- and
apply \ensuremath{\psi}\textquotesingle s adequacy to the answer. Fullness: apply the pair-table
capacity to \ensuremath{\psi}\textquotesingle s witness function, obtaining a single element s with
s\ensuremath{\cdot\langle\kappa} v, b\ensuremath{\rangle} = wit\ensuremath{\psi} v for every v and b. The obligation fires only when b
realises \ensuremath{\varphi} at v; then \ensuremath{\varphi} is forced at v by \ensuremath{\varphi}\textquotesingle s adequacy, so \ensuremath{\psi} is forced
at v since \ensuremath{\varphi \supset \psi} is forced below, and wit\ensuremath{\psi} v realises it by \ensuremath{\psi}\textquotesingle s
fullness. The table ignores its second argument entirely: the presented
world alone determines the right answer --- which is precisely the lookup
Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction} proves impossible when the world is not
presented.
\end{proof}

From Theorem 6.1 and soundness one obtains at once the \emph{refutation
lemma} (\texttt{realP\_refutes\_sequent}): every countermodel validated by the verified
checker is a \ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{p}}}-refutation of its sequent --- hypotheses realised at the
refuting world, conclusion unrealisable. Both table capacities are
discharged, with nothing assumed, by an explicit algebra of finite
lookup tables (\texttt{Tbl}, \texttt{tblPca}: base codes, formal pairs and tags, two
table formers; application is decidable lookup; the laws hold by
definitional computation), so the refutation lemma has a closed, hypothesis-free
form (\texttt{realP\_refutes\_sequent\_tbl}).

\subsection{The countermodels, finitely and without Zorn}\label{62-the-countermodels-finitely-and-without-zorn}

It remains to produce a checked countermodel for every underivable
sequent. Here the paper rejoins its parent most closely, and the reader
may find it useful to have \cite[\S4]{FairtloughMendler1997} open beside this subsection.

The canonical model there is built from worlds which are \emph{triples}
(\ensuremath{\Gamma}, \ensuremath{\Delta}, \ensuremath{\Theta}) of sets of formulas --- validated, falsified, and falsified at
every \ensuremath{R_{m}}-reachable world --- the third component being, as the original
explains, a special feature introduced because the falsity of a formula
\ensuremath{\bigcirc M} cannot be recorded by placing M, or any subformula of M, in the
other two components; it must be tracked separately. Under reading (7)
the third component is the record of a \emph{promise}: a formula the
believer has pledged never to evidence, however the constraint is
discharged. Promises are what prevent the fallible world, which
validates everything, from trivialising the \ensuremath{\forall\exists} clause. A triple is
consistent if for no finite selections \ensuremath{N_{1}},\ldots,\ensuremath{N_{n}} from \ensuremath{\Delta} and \ensuremath{K_{1}},\ldots,\ensuremath{K_{k}} from
\ensuremath{\Theta}, jointly nonempty, does

\begin{lstlisting}
  Γ ⊢ N₁ ∨ ⋯ ∨ Nₙ ∨ ◯(K₁ ∨ ⋯ ∨ Kₖ)
\end{lstlisting}

--- note the \ensuremath{\bigcirc} guarding the promises, the key subtlety of the
construction: refuting a promise-selection means deriving
somehow-one-of-the-promised, which is exactly the form that \ensuremath{\bigcirc}-elimination
can act on. The original then takes \emph{maximal}
consistent triples, by Zorn, and proves a truth lemma.

The present construction (\texttt{PLLFinComp.lean}) keeps every one of these
design decisions and changes exactly two things: the triples are finite
(\texttt{FTheory}: three finite sets, and the classical consistency notion is
reused verbatim through the evident coercion, so the entire lemma
library about the consistency formula transfers without re-proof); and
maximality is replaced by \emph{totality over the subformula closure} of the
sequent under refutation. The replacement is justified by two
observations.

The first observation (\texttt{cons\_iff\_check}) is that consistency of a
finite triple is \emph{one derivability question}: since the consistency
formula is monotone in both selections, the universally quantified
condition collapses to its worst case, the full selections. That single
question is decided by the mechanised decision procedure. Consistency
of finite triples is decidable --- and so the Lindenbaum construction can
be a computation:

\begin{lstlisting}
noncomputable def extendStep
    (T : FTheory) (φ : PLLFormula) : FTheory :=
  if (T.insVal φ).Cons
  then T.insVal φ else T.insFal φ
\end{lstlisting}

Walk the closure once; at each formula ask the decision procedure
whether believing it preserves consistency; believe if so, refute if
not. The else-branch is safe by the extension dichotomy
(\texttt{cons\_insVal\_or\_insFal}): if both extensions were inconsistent, the
two failure witnesses would combine --- by the same manipulation of selections as
in the original\textquotesingle s maximality lemma --- into an inconsistency of the
triple one started with. The result (\texttt{lindenbaum}) is a closure-total
consistent extension \emph{with the promises untouched}: the fold never
writes to the third component, an equality the truth lemma uses twice.

The second observation is that under totality the original\textquotesingle s
maximal-theory lemmas --- deductive closure, primeness of \ensuremath{\Gamma}, the
decomposition laws for falsified conjunctions, disjunctions and
implications, \ensuremath{\Theta \subseteq \Delta} --- all \emph{simplify}. Maximality entered those proofs
only through extension-failure witnesses, existentially quantified
selections needing combination; under totality every witness is a
singleton. Primeness, in full: if \ensuremath{M \vee N} is validated but neither
disjunct is, totality refutes both, and the selection \{M, N\} together
with \ensuremath{\vee}-elimination contradicts consistency directly. Five lines of
formal text against the original\textquotesingle s thirty. Constructivisation, for
once, is a simplification.

\begin{theorem}[truth lemma; \texttt{truth\_lemma}]\label{thm:truth}
On the model whose
worlds are the closure-total consistent triples, with \ensuremath{R_{i}} inclusion of
validated parts, \ensuremath{R_{m}} inclusion of validated and promised parts, the
fallible worlds those validating false, and atoms outside the closure
true everywhere: for every closure formula \ensuremath{\varphi}, membership in the
validated part forces \ensuremath{\varphi}, and membership in the falsified part refutes
it.
\end{theorem}

The propositional cases follow from the simplified lemma suite. Three cases
require a Lindenbaum extension, and they are the exact three of the
original, each start-triple carried over unchanged; only the construction
that extends them has changed. To refute a falsified \ensuremath{M \supset N} one extends
(\ensuremath{\Gamma \cup} \{M\}, \{N\}, \ensuremath{\varnothing}). To force a validated \ensuremath{\bigcirc M} at an arbitrary future one
extends (\ensuremath{\Gamma{}_{1} \cup} \{M\}, \ensuremath{\varnothing}, \ensuremath{\Theta{}_{1}}) --- the body added, the \emph{promises carried
across} --- and consistency of this triple is where the \ensuremath{\bigcirc}-guard does its work: a
violation would give \ensuremath{\Gamma{}_{1} \vdash} M \ensuremath{\supset \bigcirc}(\ensuremath{\bigvee} of a \ensuremath{\Theta{}_{1}}-selection), whence, since \ensuremath{\bigcirc M}
is validated at the future, the elimination composite yields
\ensuremath{\Gamma{}_{1} \vdash \bigcirc}(\ensuremath{\bigvee} of the selection), an inconsistency of the future with its own
promises. The extension is then a constraint-successor --- beliefs grew,
promises exactly preserved --- forcing M. To refute a falsified \ensuremath{\bigcirc M} one
extends (\ensuremath{\Gamma}, \ensuremath{\varnothing}, \{M\}): a promise \emph{created}. Its consistency reduces, by
the monotonicity composite for \ensuremath{\bigcirc}, to \ensuremath{\Gamma \vdash \bigcirc M}, contradicting the
falsification; and every constraint-successor of the extension inherits
the promise, hence --- promises being falsified, by the \ensuremath{\Theta \subseteq \Delta} law ---
refutes M. So the \ensuremath{\forall\exists} clause fails at the extension, which is an
ordinary future of the world one started at. This is the semantic role
of promises; the executable countermodels
of Section 7 display the same construction.

\begin{corollary}[\texttt{finite\_canonical\_countermodel},
\texttt{emitter\_completeness}]\label{cor:emitter}
Every underivable sequent is refuted at a
world of its finite canonical model; enumerating the (finitely many)
worlds yields the countermodel as literal checker data:

\begin{lstlisting}
¬ (Γ ⊢ C)  implies  ∃ M w, checkB M w Γ C = true
\end{lstlisting}
\end{corollary}

Consider what has been achieved axiomatically. No maximality
principle appears anywhere above, and after the axiom-hygiene pass the
entire chain of this subsection --- the decidability of consistency, the
decided Lindenbaum fold (now, in fact, a computable function), the
truth lemma, and the countermodel enumeration --- audits at
\texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}: no choice (what little classicality
survives in the artefact is a statement-vocabulary matter, discussed
in Section 9). It is tempting, and would be wrong, to say that the
logic proves its own completeness: the proof
is carried out in the metatheory, and a quantifier-free propositional logic
cannot even state completeness. What is true, and we think worth
saying carefully, is that the metatheoretic proof\textquotesingle s only oracle is the
object logic\textquotesingle s own decision procedure --- an arrangement made possible by
the fact, special to a decidable logic with no embedded arithmetic,
that there is no Gödelian obstruction to consulting it.

\begin{remark}[the apparent circle]\label{rem:circle}
Decidability before completeness has an air of circularity, since the
familiar route runs the other way: the original paper\textquotesingle s Theorem 2.8
obtained decidability \emph{from} completeness, through the finite
model property, and a decidability so obtained could not be used inside
a completeness proof without vicious regress. The present arrangement
is innocent, for three reasons. First, the decision procedure\textquotesingle s
correctness is a fact of proof theory alone --- the repaired
terminating calculus proves exactly the PLL sequents, and the
loop-checked search decides that calculus; no model is mentioned
anywhere in the chain. Second, the completeness proof consumes
decidability only as a \emph{derivability} oracle, never a semantic
one: what the oracle contributes is the totality of the case analysis
``believe or refute''. The classical proof settles this by Zorn\textquotesingle s lemma and
excluded middle; here it is settled by a computation. The semantics enters
only afterwards, in the truth lemma. Third --- an argument available
only to a mechanised development --- the absence of the circle is
itself machine-checked: the import graph is acyclic by construction,
the completeness modules import the decidability module, and the
decidability module imports no semantic module whatever. The reversal
has a respectable pedigree: decidability of intuitionistic
propositional logic \cite{Gentzen1935} preceded its Kripke
completeness~\cite{Kripke1965} by thirty years, and for the same
reason: a terminating calculus is a purely syntactic object.
Completeness then shows that this syntactic calculus suffices
to build every countermodel; the dependency runs in one direction only, and the
module import graph enforces it.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The theorem}\label{63-the-theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{derivable\_iff\_no\_realP\_refutation}]\label{thm:crown}
A sequent is derivable in PLL exactly
when no token-decorated, checker-verified finite model
\ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{p}}}-realisability-refutes it:

\begin{lstlisting}
Nonempty (LaxND Γ C) ↔
  ¬ ∃ (M : FinCM) (w : Nat)
      (hwf : M.WellFormed) (hlt : w < M.n),
    (∀ ψ ∈ Γ, ∃ x,
        x ⊩ᵖ[Ev₀, tblκ, ⟨w, hlt⟩] ψ) ∧
    ¬ ∃ x, x ⊩ᵖ[Ev₀, tblκ, ⟨w, hlt⟩] C
  -- Ev₀ := tokenEvidence tblPca M hwf tok₀
\end{lstlisting}
\end{theorem}

Forward: soundness together with Theorem~\ref{thm:adequacy-fullness} --- a derivable conclusion
is forced wherever its realised hypotheses are, hence realised by its
own fullness witness. Backward: Corollary~\ref{cor:emitter} composed
with the closed form of the refutation lemma. Every quantifier ranges over concrete objects --- finite models
as printable data, one token per atom, realisers in an explicit algebra
of lookup tables --- and the backward case decision is made by the
decision procedure, not by excluded middle. The theorem audits at
\texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}, enforced by build-time guards: the
completeness of PLL for realisability is, in the formal sense the audit
measures, a choice-free theorem. PLL is complete for the semantics in
which evidence may react to the future; incomplete, by Section 5, for
every more rigid discipline.

\section{The theory, run}\label{7-the-theory-run}

\emph{(This section\textquotesingle s artefacts are all generated: countermodels by
evaluation of the emitter, proof terms by the kernel; each is
re-checkable from the cited files.)}

The completeness theorem\textquotesingle s countermodels are not merely finite but
executable, and the artefact exploits this in both directions. An
untrusted search procedure (\texttt{CounterEmit.emit}) proposes countermodels
from failed proof search; the verified checker certifies them; a wrong
proposal can produce only a failure, never a wrong certificate. In the
other direction, any certified model refutes in the full realisability
sense, by the refutation lemma.

That discipline is packaged as a single entry point.
\texttt{PLLSearch.lean} attempts a sequent \emph{either way} --- a
certified battery of small frames and the closure emitter for
refutations, a fuel-free backward searcher over the terminating calculus
for proofs --- and returns a verdict carrying its own certificate: a
proof term the typechecker validates, or a finite model the verified
checker accepts. The third verdict is an explicit \texttt{unknown},
which asserts nothing. Soundness is therefore structural rather than a
property of the search code: a wrong internal guess can only degrade a
verdict to \texttt{unknown}, never manufacture a false one. This is the
development\textquotesingle s practical engine, and the division of
labour is deliberate. The decision procedure behind
Remark~\ref{rem:circle} is constructive and total, but its fuel bounds
are far too large to run on any but trivial sequents; proof extraction
and automation therefore use the searcher and keep only its
certificates. The cost profile is recorded honestly alongside it:
successes are cheap, the worst case is exponential --- provability is
already PSPACE-hard for the \ensuremath{\bigcirc}-free fragment --- and
the frame battery is incomplete by design, a first filter rather than a
complete refuter.

For \ensuremath{\bigcirc p \vdash} p the emitter produces a five-world model, pinned in the
artefact as data (\texttt{demoM}), whose worlds read as belief states: a state
believing exactly \ensuremath{\bigcirc p} (the refuting world); above it a state where the
constraint has been discharged and p is believed; the fallible top world;
and a state that has \emph{promised} never to
evidence p, whose only constraint-successor is itself. The
kernel-checked certificate

\begin{lstlisting}
theorem somehow_p_not_p :
    ¬ Nonempty
        (LaxND [(prop "p").somehow] (prop "p")) :=
  FinCM.not_provable_of_check
    (M := demoM) (w := 2) (by decide)
\end{lstlisting}

runs the verified checker inside the kernel; no compiled evaluation is
trusted, and the audit is choice-free. Under reading (7), believing \ensuremath{\bigcirc p}
cannot be converted into believing p, and the countermodel shows why: there
is a coherent belief state in which the constraint\textquotesingle s discharge
remains forever pending. Figure~\ref{fig:demoM} is the model, drawn
by the artefact from its own data.

\begin{remark}[preorder, not partial order]\label{rem:preorder}
In all these models \ensuremath{R_{i}} is a genuine preorder: distinct worlds can be
mutually related. The twenty-world model has thirteen such pairs, among
them the refuting world and its two critical futures --- \ensuremath{w_{4}}, \ensuremath{w_{5}}, \ensuremath{w_{6}}
believe exactly the same formulas and differ only in their standing
promises, so the refutation just displayed takes place entirely
inside a single \ensuremath{R_{i}}-equivalence class. This reflects the
construction: canonical \ensuremath{R_{i}} compares beliefs and deliberately ignores
promises; refuting a \ensuremath{\bigcirc}-formula changes no belief --- it only adds
a commitment. \ensuremath{R_{m}}, which carries promises along, is antisymmetric in
every model of this section, and nothing is lost for validity: forcing
is hereditary along \ensuremath{R_{i}} (\texttt{force\_hered}), minimisation removes
the equivalent twins, and both minimised models above are posets
(build-time guards \texttt{riEquiv}/\texttt{rmEquiv},
\texttt{PLLDiagram.lean}). One design observation, recorded without
mechanised warrant: a canonical \ensuremath{R_{i}} refined to respect promises ---
antisymmetric by construction --- would obstruct the \ensuremath{\bigcirc}-case of the
truth lemma, whose refuting step needs a same-belief future in which
the old promises have lapsed and one fresh promise stands. The preorder
is therefore not an accident of the construction but is required by the
\ensuremath{\bigcirc}-case of the truth lemma.
\end{remark}

Finally, evidence in the positive direction. The sequent
\ensuremath{\bigcirc A}, \ensuremath{\bigcirc}(\ensuremath{A \supset B}) \ensuremath{\vdash \bigcirc B} --- combine a belief with a believed implication --- has
the kernel-checked proof term

\begin{lstlisting}
let val• := #0 in (let val• := #2 in val (#0 #1))
\end{lstlisting}

two monadic binds and an application: run the belief in A, obtaining
evidence under its constraint; under that constraint run the belief in
\ensuremath{A \supset B}; apply; return under the composed constraint. The uniform
extraction of the artefact (\texttt{extract\_sound}) compiles this derivation,
by the equations ``laxIntro is the identity, laxElim is
abstraction-then-application'', to the polynomial

\begin{lstlisting}
(λa. (λg. g · a) · f) · x
\end{lstlisting}

whose value \ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{u}}}-realises the conclusion wherever the context is
realised; the strategy extraction compiles the same derivation to the
reactive form in which the presented future is fed to the first
strategy, the named witness it returns is where the second strategy is
run, and the results compose. Sequential composition of beliefs is the
meet law of the evidence semantics (\texttt{ob\_strength}), and the artefact
records --- one of the development\textquotesingle s early machine-checked
findings --- that no confluence assumption appears anywhere: belief
composition is sequential, not confluent.

A second example, this time negative.
Idempotence \ensuremath{\bigcirc\bigcirc}M \ensuremath{\dashv\vdash \bigcirc M} is two proof terms: the multiplication
\ensuremath{\mu} = \ensuremath{\lambda}x. let val \ensuremath{y \Leftarrow x} in y and the unit \ensuremath{\eta} = \ensuremath{\lambda}x. val x. Are they
mutually inverse?

\begin{proposition}[\texttt{mu\_eta\_not\_mutually\_inverse},
\texttt{eta\_mu\_not\_conv\_id}; \texttt{PLLIdempotency.lean}]\label{prop:mueta}
\ensuremath{\mu \circ \eta} reduces to the identity; \ensuremath{\eta \circ \mu} is not even convertible
to it. Idempotence is inter-derivability, not isomorphism.
\end{proposition}

The first composite is the monad\textquotesingle s unit law, holding as computation
(the let fires on the val); the second normalises to
\ensuremath{\lambda}x. val (let val \ensuremath{y \Leftarrow x} in y), a stuck term in which the collapse
remains pending inside the re-assertion, and the artefact computes the
composite\textquotesingle s complete reduction graph --- four terms, closed under
reduction, the identity not among them. The separation holds in the
strongest sense the calculus can express: the reduction is confluent
(\texttt{PLLConfluence.lean} --- Newman\textquotesingle s lemma~\cite{Newman1942} from
strong normalisation, the critical pairs being \texttt{let}-\ensuremath{\beta} and
\texttt{let}-assoc overlapping at a \texttt{bind}), so normal forms are
unique, conversion is decided by the normaliser, and the failure is of
convertibility, not merely of reduction. Read doxastically, collapsing
a belief about a belief and then re-asserting belief in the result is
not a null operation on evidence, though it is one on truth.

One asymmetry visible in these examples is worth noting.
Evidence extracted from proofs is \emph{frame-uniform}: one polynomial
serves every model. The fullness witnesses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:adequacy-fullness} are
\emph{frame-aware}: tables built by surveying the finite frame. Provable
formulas have evidence needing no knowledge of the situation; merely
true ones have evidence only relative to a survey of this situation\textquotesingle s
futures. The idealisation Hintikka identified appears exactly in the
completeness theorem\textquotesingle s omniscient survey: stated explicitly, and confined to
where it belongs.

Finally, a first applied step from the programme that motivated
PLL~\cite{FairtloughMendler1994}.
Mendler\textquotesingle s timing analysis of
combinational circuits~\cite{Mendler2000} reads a proof as a
stabilisation bound --- unit: a wire, delay 0; multiplication:
sequential composition, +; strength: reconvergent merge, max --- and
the artefact evaluates PLL proof terms in exactly this delay algebra
(\texttt{PLLTiming.lean}, \texttt{PLLTimingAdder.lean},
\texttt{PLLTimingRipple.lean}, \texttt{PLLTimingLookahead.lean}). The
reconvergent-fanout circuit of \cite[\S7]{Mendler2000} is reproduced
with its false-path result --- on the input class holding a = 1 the
extracted bound is strictly below the topological longest path,
because a proof traverses only real logical dependencies --- and a
four-bit carry-skip adder is certified faster than its topological
bound (\texttt{falsePath\_beats\_topological},
\texttt{skip\_beats\_topological}; both with \emph{no
axioms at all}). These false-path results are one side of a dichotomy the artefact now
makes precise. Extraction never exceeds the topological longest path;
the interest is in when it falls strictly below. The carry-ripple
adder is the tight extreme, and it is now treated at \emph{every}
width: one reusable carry cell applied \ensuremath{n} times to the
carry-in --- so that the proof term is literally the cell iterated
\ensuremath{n} times --- has extracted delay exactly
\ensuremath{n\cdot\delta}, by induction on the width
(\texttt{ready\_bound}), and this \emph{equals} the topological path
(\texttt{ripple\_tight}), a single carry chain having no false path to
discard. With the carry-skip adder strictly below, the two
\emph{bracket} the general fact --- proof-term extraction is at most
topological analysis, with equality exactly when the longest path is
sensitisable. The same induction exhibits the extracted delay as the
nesting depth of the carry cell in the proof: critical-path length is
proof depth.

That identity turns circuit optimisation into proof restructuring, and
the artefact certifies the paradigm case. A carry-lookahead adder
produces the same group carry as the ripple by folding the per-bit
generate/propagate pairs under their associative merge as a
\emph{balanced tree} rather than a line. Abstracting a pair as one lax
signal and the merge as a single reusable cell, the balanced fold of
depth \ensuremath{k} and the ripple-shaped fold of length
\ensuremath{2^{k}-1} are two proof terms of the \emph{same} sequent
built from the \emph{same} \ensuremath{2^{k}-1} merge cells;
re-association alone drops the delay from
\ensuremath{(2^{k}-1)\cdot\delta} to \ensuremath{k\cdot\delta} --- linear
to logarithmic in the leaf count, with no extra hardware
(\texttt{lookahead\_beats\_ripple}). That the two networks compute the
\emph{same} carry is proved, not assumed: the extractor is parametric in
its value layer, so running the same two proof terms with the carry pair
in place of the trivial layer returns the same group
generate/propagate pair, for any associative merge and hence for the
carry algebra itself (\texttt{balGP\_eq\_linGP},
\texttt{lookahead\_eq\_ripple\_gp}, with the merge\textquotesingle s
associativity checked axiom-free); a single evaluation yields both that
value and the delay. Ripple is the deepest proof of the group carry,
lookahead a shallow one, and the speed-up is precisely the reduction in
\ensuremath{\bigcirc}-nesting depth --- a re-association of the proof, not
a change in what is proved.

The two remaining circuits of the case-study list are asynchronous, and
between them they display one law of the modality that holds and one
that fails. Muller\textquotesingle s \emph{C-element} waits for both of
its inputs; logically it is the meet law \ensuremath{\bigcirc A \wedge
\bigcirc B \vdash \bigcirc(A \wedge B)}, which is derivable. Its
canonical derivation is sequential --- two binds, so the multiplication
charges \ensuremath{\delta_{a} + \delta_{b}} --- while the element
realises the same law through the strength, charging
\ensuremath{\max(\delta_{a}, \delta_{b}) + \delta_{C}}. The element is
thus a cheaper realisation of a derivable law, and it pays exactly when
\ensuremath{\delta_{C} < \min(\delta_{a}, \delta_{b})}
(\texttt{cElem\_beats\_sequential}). An \emph{arbiter} grants one of two
competing requests. Under contention it believes that some grant occurs,
\ensuremath{\bigcirc(g_{1} \vee g_{2})}, while believing of neither grant
that it occurs, since nothing in the inputs determines which way the
resolution falls; that is exactly the failure of distribution over
\ensuremath{\vee}, and it is refuted by a three-world model read as the
arbiter itself (\texttt{arbiter\_no\_distribution}, choice-free). The
reason is structural: mutual exclusion means the two grant-futures never
reconverge, so the frame is not mutually confluent
(\texttt{arbiter\_not\_confluent}), and mutually confluent frames are
exactly the models of PLL with the distribution scheme
(\texttt{derivU\_iff\_confluent\_valid}). That the arbiter may not grant
twice is the circuit-level reason the scheme fails. The model is the
split model of \cite[Fig.~3]{FairtloughMendler1997} once more, now with
a reading: contention below, the two resolutions above. Under reading (7) these
are all belief computations: a signal\textquotesingle s delay is the cost
of discharging the constraint under which its stability is believed,
and re-associating a proof of equal size into a shallower one is how
that cost is cut.

We are explicit about what this is and is not: it is the delay-algebra
and proof-theoretic half of the programme\textquotesingle s first case
study --- extraction and refutation in the constraint semantics, not in
the evidence semantics of Sections 4--6. The study itself --- these same
netlists with their stabilises-to specifications formalised
\emph{in the evidence semantics}, the components composed into a larger
netlist under a formally combined specification,
\ensuremath{\bigcirc}(out = spec) proved there and its evidence term
extracted --- requires a realiser layer that is designed but not
implemented; a staged design brief accompanies the artefact, and the
programme --- timing at scale, proof-carrying authorization, certified
effectful computation --- is the subject of a companion paper in
preparation.

\section{Related work}\label{8-related-work}

The nearest neighbour of reading (7) in the literature is the
intuitionistic epistemic logic of Artemov and Protopopescu~\cite{ArtemovProtopopescu2016}, whose
belief system IEL\ensuremath{{}^{-}} adopts co-reflection A \ensuremath{\supset} KA and rejects factivity,
so that K false is consistent, exactly as \ensuremath{\bigcirc\mathit{false}} is here. PLL
adds idempotence; we conjecture PLL-\ensuremath{\bigcirc} is exactly IEL\ensuremath{{}^{-}} extended by
\ensuremath{\bigcirc\bigcirc}A \ensuremath{\supset \bigcirc A}, and record the question as open. IEL itself has
been mechanised, in Coq~\cite{HagemeierKirst2022}. Justification
logic~\cite{Artemov2001,Artemov2008,ArtemovFitting2019} in its
forgetful reading forces factivity and is therefore not this.

Two classical traditions are illuminated by the collapse theorem.
Doxastic logic in the Hintikka--KD45
tradition~\cite{Hintikka1962,FaginHalpernMosesVardi1995,Stalnaker2006}
is the
baseline against which Proposition~\ref{prop:profile} should be read:
the lax believer is fully introspective but has no D: an
inconsistent belief is admitted here, where KD45 forbids it. The evidence
models of the classical belief-revision
literature~\cite{VanBenthemPacuit2011,BaltagBezhanishviliOzgunSmets2016,
BaltagBezhanishviliOzgunSmets2022}
build belief from evidence with interior-type operators over a
\emph{topology}; Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} says why that extra
structure is obligatory --- on the bare classical algebra every nucleus
is closed, so evidence must live somewhere else. And a security
literature arrived at the two signature facts of Section 3
independently, in doxastic vocabulary: in the authorization logics
descended from the dependency core
calculus~\cite{Abadi2006,GargAbadi2008}, the modality ``K says''
validates A \ensuremath{\supset} (K says A) --- every principal believes what is
true --- and (K says false) does not entail false: a principal may
assert the absurd without collapsing the logic. A lax modality read as
an agent\textquotesingle s attitude --- truth-closed, and admitting an inconsistent
belief without collapse --- is already used in engineering practice.

The constraint reading of the parent paper and the monadic reading of
computational type theory~\cite{Moggi1991,BentonBiermanDePaiva1998} are
the two established computational faces
of \ensuremath{\bigcirc}; the doxastic reading is compatible with both, and the
double-negation believer --- in constraint models with \ensuremath{R_{m}} = \ensuremath{R_{i}} and no
fallible worlds, \ensuremath{\bigcirc M} is forced exactly where \ensuremath{\lnot\lnot}M is (machine-checked,
\texttt{force\_somehow\_iff\_notnot}) --- recovers the continuation reading~\cite{Krivine2009} as a
special case. On the mechanised side, the normalisation-by-evaluation
line for modal and lax calculi in
Agda~\cite{ValliappanRuchTomeCortinas2022,Valliappan2026} is the
closest packaged artefact to our term-calculus layer, and the relation
is technical, not merely bibliographical. That semantics interprets
the lax modality by a \emph{bare existential} over the constraint
relation --- a clause that is not hereditary by itself --- and
accordingly assumes \emph{forward confluence} of the two accessibility
relations; the parent paper\textquotesingle s \ensuremath{\forall\exists} clause
internalises heredity and assumes nothing. The two clause styles are
reconciled exactly on the mutually confluent frames of Section 10:
there the \ensuremath{\forall\exists} clause collapses to the bare
existential (\texttt{force\_somehow\_iff\_of\_confluent}), and the
logic of that frame class is precisely PLL +
\ensuremath{\bigcirc}(A \ensuremath{\vee} B) \ensuremath{\supset}
(\ensuremath{\bigcirc}A \ensuremath{\vee} \ensuremath{\bigcirc}B)
(\texttt{derivU\_iff\_confluent\_valid}) --- a scheme PLL does not
prove. The simpler clause is therefore bought at the price of a
strictly stronger logic. Nothing in the evidence semantics of
Sections 4--6 assumes confluence anywhere; in the one place it might
seem needed --- the meet law composing two beliefs --- the mechanised
proof is sequential composition, discharging the second constraint at
the world the first produced (Section 7), never a common refinement of
independent successors. We are not aware
of a prior completeness theorem for a
lax logic with respect to a realisability semantics, nor of a prior
mechanised canonical-model construction whose Lindenbaum step is an
appeal to the object logic\textquotesingle s decision procedure. The
terminating-calculus route to decidability for PLL is due to
Iemhoff~\cite{Iemhoff2024}, resting on a general G3i-to-G4i
transfer~\cite{Iemhoff2022}, with earlier proof search for PLL due to
Howe~\cite{Howe2001}; the artefact contains a machine-checked
counterexample to the published calculus and a repaired one,
communicated separately.

\section{Method}\label{9-method}

Every claim of this paper is a theorem of a Lean
4~\cite{DeMouraUllrich2021} development~\cite{BeliefArtefact}, over
Mathlib~\cite{MathlibCommunity2020}, that also contains its parent
paper entire. The axiom footprint of each
result is recorded: nothing at all for a handful of results (among them
a well-foundedness theorem for the Kleene--Brouwer
order~\cite{Kechris1995}, a standalone
fully constructive result in the artefact); \texttt{[propext]} or
\texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]} --- no choice --- for the proof theory, the
decoration half of completeness, the obstruction, and the executable
countermodel certificates, the decidability of the logic, the finitised
canonical model, and the completeness theorem itself; classical axioms
remain only in the parent\textquotesingle s Zorn completeness (kept deliberately, as
the foil) and in a handful of statements whose Mathlib vocabulary
(\texttt{List.toFinset}, \texttt{Finset.toList}, the lattice
\ensuremath{\cup}/\ensuremath{\cap}) is choice-tainted at definition level --- an audit
walks the bodies of the constants a \emph{statement} mentions, so such
a phrasing can never audit clean, however its proof is conducted; each
is a one- or two-line wrapper around a clean restatement through a
choice-free finite-set toolkit.

Two remarks on what the method made feasible. The axiom-hygiene pass
itself --- re-founding a completed completeness proof choice-free, by
replacing a classical finite-set vocabulary, a classical decidability
instance and a classical tactic with constructive carriers throughout
a multi-thousand-line development --- is the kind of change that would
be hard to justify, or to referee, done by hand; with the proof
assistant tracking the invariants it was routine. And one metatheoretic
result appears to be new to the mechanised literature in passing: the
strong normalisation of Section 2 is, as far as we can determine, the
first mechanised \ensuremath{\top\top}-lifting with sums --- the sole prior
mechanisation of strong normalisation for the computational
metalanguage treats the core calculus without sums, in
Isabelle/HOL-Nominal~\cite{DoczkalSchwinghammer2009}, and the
paper-form treatments of sums~\cite{Lindley2005,LindleyStark2005} are
unmechanised; cf.\ the POPLMark Reloaded
benchmark~\cite{AbelEtAl2019} for mechanised logical-relations
normalisation.

We have already said that the formalisation functioned as an
instrument. It is worth saying how. Three intended theorems failed, and
their failures were themselves established: a collapse lemma for \ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{s}}}
(refuted; the reformulation became the refutation lemma), the claim that \ensuremath{\bigcirc} is
not a normal modality (refuted; it is normal, every nucleus validating
K), and the intended completeness of \ensuremath{\Vdash^{\mathrm{s}}}, whose failure is
Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction} and whose repair is the paper\textquotesingle s central
definition. A referee may
reasonably ask what in this paper was found by the human author and
what by the machine; the honest answer, recorded commit-by-commit in
the artefact\textquotesingle s history, is that the direction, the reading, the
standards and the corrections were human, and a large part of the
construction, the drafting and two of the three failures were found by
a language model operating the proof assistant under those standards.
Section 9.1 states this precisely.

\section{Conclusion and open problems}\label{10-conclusion-and-open-problems}

However strange a belief modality without factivity may at first
appear, it is a natural object, known already from half a dozen
mathematical contexts; what the present paper adds is that its logic of
evidential belief is non-degenerate exactly over intuitionistic logic,
and that PLL is precisely complete for the evidence discipline in which
realisers meet the future as an input. The countermodels witnessing the
``precisely'' are finite, executable, and readable as belief states; the
canonical construction that produces them is the parent paper\textquotesingle s, with
Zorn\textquotesingle s lemma traded for the logic\textquotesingle s own decision procedure.

Open problems. The identification of PLL\ensuremath{{}^{\mathrm{u}}},
the logic of rigid evidence --- for which the natural candidate
axiomatisation is the distribution scheme itself. The parent
paper\textquotesingle s Theorem 4.5 ties
\ensuremath{\bigcirc}(A \ensuremath{\vee} B) \ensuremath{\supset}
(\ensuremath{\bigcirc}A \ensuremath{\vee} \ensuremath{\bigcirc}B) to
\emph{mutual confluence} of the two accessibility relations --- from
any world, an \ensuremath{R_{m}}-step and an \ensuremath{R_{i}}-step
close at a common world --- under which the \ensuremath{\forall\exists}
clause collapses to bare possibility, so that credibility, once
attained, is already stable. The artefact now carries both
sides. Soundness: the collapse
(\texttt{force\_somehow\_iff\_of\_confluent}, no axioms at all), the
validity of the scheme on confluent models
(\texttt{force\_somehow\_or\_dist\_of\_confluent}), and the fact that
the split model refuting the scheme fails confluence exactly
(\texttt{modelOrSplit\_not\_confluent}). And completeness, proved
here: \emph{PLL extended by the scheme --- taken as additional
hypotheses --- is sound and complete for mutually confluent constraint
models} (\texttt{derivU\_iff\_confluent\_valid},
\texttt{PLLConfluentComplete.lean}), by a canonical model whose worlds
are the deductively closed prime sets of formulas, in which
\ensuremath{\bigcirc^{-1}} of a prime set is prime \emph{precisely
because of the scheme}, and mutual confluence holds with
\ensuremath{\bigcirc^{-1}} of the \ensuremath{R_{i}}-successor as
witness; no promise component is needed anywhere, because on confluent
frames the clause is already bare possibility.
