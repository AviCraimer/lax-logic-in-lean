Minimal forms

Found by enumerating ◯-free formulas by truth set. The nodes themselves are labeled t n, and t(n) is the smallest known representative of that class — same class, smaller formula. The minimal recursion, valid from k ≥ 1 over the base t(1) = ◯⊥, t(2) = ¬◯⊥, t(3) = t(1) ∨ t(2), t(4) = t(2) ⊃ t(1), is —

t(2k+4) = t(2k+2) ⊃ t(2k−1)
t(2k+3) = t(2k) ∨ t(2k+2)

— it never re-expands the large odd rung, where the textbook recursion rn(2k+4) = rn(2k+3) ⊃ rn(2k+1) does; both weights, minimal and textbook, appear in the readout when you click a node. t 8–t 13 come out 2–4× smaller. Click a node.

Visible points

A point a names a point of the Esakia dual directly iff a is join-prime: a ≤ x ∨ y ⟹ a ≤ x or a ≤ y. Tested here by brute force over every pair of rungs shown — recomputed live as N changes.

Why six points are PROVED

wip/visible.lean (sorry-free, landed adb7ad5) proves join-primality outright — not just verified-in-scope — for ⊤, t1, t2, t4, t6, by way of a class of formulas called Harrop (no ∨ in a strictly positive position): every Harrop formula is join-prime, because a cut-free derivation of a hypothesis-Harrop sequent ending in x ∨ y can only ever end in orR1 or orR2 (harrop_sc, primeAll_of_harrop).

Mechanism 1 — the box helps for free. ◯A is Harrop for arbitrary A, not only Harrop A: the lax left rule laxL has a succedent restricted to ◯-formulas, so it can never fire when the goal is a disjunction — a boxed hypothesis can never help produce a disjunction. This is stronger than the plain-IPC analogy predicts (◯ has no IPC counterpart), and it is what makes t1 = ◯⊥, t2 = ¬◯⊥ and t4 = (◯⊥ ∨ ¬◯⊥) ⊃ ◯⊥ immediate.

Mechanism 2 — rescue by interderivability. t6 is not itself Harrop — its consequent t3 = ◯⊥ ∨ ¬◯⊥ is a disjunction — but join-primality is a property of the interderivability class (PrimeAll.of_interd), and t6 ⊣⊢ ¬¬◯⊥ ⊃ ◯⊥ (rnSub_six_interd), whose consequent is Harrop. The underlying law is axiom-free and general in A: ((A∨¬A) ∨ ((A∨¬A) ⊃ A)) ⊃ (A∨¬A)  ⊣⊢  ¬¬A ⊃ A (interd_rn6_dneg).

No floor either

Height (the ladder climbs forever) and width (the gap antichain, infinite) are only two of the three ways RN(◯,{}) is infinite. The third is depth: the finite meets of the gap family, Gmeet n = g1 ∧ g2 ∧ … ∧ g(n+1), form a chain that descends strictly forever (Gmeet_le, Gmeet_desc_strict, wip/uiObstruct.lean) with no floor — no formula sits below every g k, in PLL or even restricted to variable-free formulas (gap_no_glb, L_no_greatest, wip/floor.lean). RN(◯,{}) is not a complete lattice. Drawn as a new column in the Families tab, below g1.

The invisible points

Every a gives a principal filter ↑a = {x : a ≤ x} — always a filter, proper iff a ≠ ⊥. It is prime — names a point of the Esakia dual X directly — iff a is join-prime (the rings and dots on the diagrams). Prime filters that are not of this principal form are the invisible points: points of X that no formula names.

Existence. X is compact (Esakia/Priestley duality); a compact space that is also discrete is finite; RN(◯,{}) is infinite — the rungs escape forever, so the algebra never stabilises (exists_escaping_rungs, closed_lax_infinite, PLLLaxInfinite.lean) — so X is not finite, hence not discrete. Non-principal prime filters are the general shape of what a non-discrete point looks like; the gap family below is a concrete one.

The gap family. Each g k = ◯t(2k+1) ⊃ t(2k+1) names a clopen upset φ(g k). The intersection ⋂ₖ φ(g k) is a closed upset of X — an intersection of clopens — and it is nonempty: every finite sub-intersection is φ(Gmeet n) for a genuine, non-⊥ class of RN(◯,{}), and compactness turns that finite-intersection property into a nonempty limit. It is not clopen: gap_no_glb proves no formula derives every g k while being derived by every other common lower bound — over all of PLL — and its variable-free reading L_no_greatest says the same for RN(◯,{}) itself (both wip/floor.lean, sorry-free). Dually: no clopen upset inside ⋂ₖ φ(g k) is the largest one — if one were, its formula would be the missing glb. So some points of that closed set are unnamed by any formula: the missing interpolant would have to live at one of them.

Open. Is the filter ⋃ₙ ↑Gmeet n itself prime — equivalently, does ⋂ₖ φ(g k) have a least point? If so, the whole descending chain converges on one invisible point. Nothing here settles it.

Completing the order. RN(◯,{}) is not a complete lattice — gap_no_glb/L_no_greatest are proofs that a specific infimum is missing. The standard way to name what an order is missing is an order completion: MacNeille's is the smallest one, adding exactly the joins and meets needed, as new elements of a larger lattice. It is usually credited to Funayama (1959, Nagoya Math. J. 15, 71–81) that the MacNeille completion of a Heyting algebra is again a Heyting algebra — in fact a complete one, a frame. Checked here against a citable secondary source, Bezhanishvili, Gabelaia & Jibladze, "Funayama's theorem revisited" (Algebra Universalis 70 (2013), 271–286), who state that the variety of all Heyting algebras is one of only three varieties closed under MacNeille completion — not checked against Funayama's original 1959 paper directly, and not checked against anything in this repository: nothing here computes or names the MacNeille completion of RN(◯,{}).

This does not give uniform interpolation. MacNeille completion adds the missing meet as a new element of a bigger lattice — not a formula of PLL. Uniform interpolation demands an actual formula, inside the algebra RN(◯,{}) already has. Order-completeness names what is missing; uniform interpolation demands that nothing be missing. gap_no_glb/L_no_greatest are an order-completeness failure, and they obstruct uniform interpolation exactly because the missing interpolant would have to be that missing meet, written as a formula — and by these theorems, no such formula exists anywhere in PLL.

A different completion. The completion uniform interpolation actually corresponds to is Robinson's model completion — a model-theoretic notion, about the first-order theory of a whole variety of algebras, not about filling gaps in one particular order. Pitts (1992, J. Symbolic Logic 57, 33–52) proved IPC has uniform interpolation, with ∀p, ∃p as adjoints to substitution; Ghilardi & Zawadowski recast this as: a variety of Heyting or modal algebras has uniform interpolation exactly when the first-order theory of that variety admits a model completion — the positive case for Heyting algebras/IPC, the negative case for S4, where they prove no model completion exists (Sheaves, Games, and Model Completions, Trends in Logic 14, Springer 2002; "Undefinability of Propositional Quantifiers in the Modal System S4", Studia Logica 55 (1995), 259–271). Reported here from that literature, independent of the order-completion discussion above and not checked against this repository — the two completions should not be run together.

The classification, proved

Every ◯-free one-variable formula is, after p ↦ ◯⊥, interderivable with a rung or with ⊤ (image_classification, sorry-free). So "off the rungs and not ⊤" now literally means outside im h — the caveat on c1 (was q5), c3 = ◯t7 (was ◯q11) and the chain is gone, and it also decides cells: t6 ⊬ t4 (was q10 ⊬ q6) falls out with no countermodel (q10_not_q6).

Dictionary — 15 classes + the ◯-chain

The order is now settled in 224 of 225 cells — the last open cell is r1 ⊢ ◯g1 (was q14 ⊢ q13). c2 ⊢ t7 (was q12 ⊢ q11) fell to the non-confluent five-world battery, which also produced the biggest structural news: c3 (= ◯t7) is a new class (cBox11_settled), strictly above t7, so the dictionary is not closed under ◯. Beyond it stretches an infinite strictly ascending chain — the boxed odd rungs c1 = ◯t3 < c2 = ◯t5 < c3 = ◯t7 < …, strict at every step (chain_step_strict), pairwise distinct — now drawn at the right edge of the diagram. Since the classification im h = rungs ∪ {⊤} is mechanised (image_classification), every one of them is off the image outright: the complement of the ladder image is infinite, with no caveat (complement_infinite_final). The width question is settled too: the collapse statements g k = c k ⊃ t(2k+1) — level-k generalisations of g1 (was q8, gap_one_q8) — are pairwise incomparable for k ≥ 2 (width_infinite), so RN(◯,{}) holds an infinite antichain alongside the infinite chain: its width is unbounded, and each g k is a further new class, off the image and off the chain (gap_off_image, gap_not_chain). The orange edge is s1 < c2 (was q9 < q12): since ◯(t3 ∨ t4) = c2 while ◯t3 ∨ ◯t4 = s1, that strict gap is the failure of distribution, refuted by a five-world countermodel (distr_4_3_not_derivable).

Level-1 accidents

t5 ⊢ g1, t4 ⊢ g1 and c1 ⊢ t6 hold at level 1 and provably fail from level 2 (odd_not_le_gap, even_not_le_gap, chain_not_le_even). They are the dotted grey edges: level 1 is not a typical level, and reading the general shape off it is exactly the mistake these three theorems close.

Open

The co-gaps g k ⊃ c k: strictly above c k (dC_not_le_chain), off the image — their order structure with each other is open. Also open: r k against w k, and everything at the levels this picture does not show.

The descending chain — no floor

Below g1 hangs a new column: the finite meets Gmeet n = g1 ∧ g2 ∧ … ∧ g(n+1). Each is strictly below the last — Gmeet(n+1) ⊢ Gmeet n but not conversely (Gmeet_le, Gmeet_desc_strict, wip/uiObstruct.lean) — an infinite strictly descending chain, the mirror image of the ascending ◯-chain in the Dictionary tab.

It has no floor: no formula lies below every Gmeet n, in the whole of PLL, let alone variable-free (gap_no_glb, L_no_greatest, wip/floor.lean) — so RN(◯,{}) is not a complete lattice, even though the antichain alone has a common lower bound (t3, t3_below_gap) and every individual meet exists as a formula. (g2 ⊢ g1 — gap_two_le_one — is why the antichain proper is drawn from k = 2: g1 is strictly above it.)

What this misses is a point of the Esakia dual, not just a formula — see "The invisible points" in the Dictionary tab: the closed set ⋂ₖ φ(g k) is nonempty by compactness but not clopen, and completing the order (MacNeille) is not the same question as uniform interpolation.

Families — the combs

One picture of the whole known structure. The green spine is the ladder — the odd rungs t1, t3, t5, t7, t9 in a chain, the even rungs hanging left. Off each odd rung t(2k+1) hangs a comb: the six families, all of them defined from that rung, all of them repeating verbatim at every level k ≥ 1. What the picture is for is the contrast between the two kinds of column — the blue columns (c, s) are joined bottom to top because they are chains, the amber columns (g, r, ◯g) carry a bracket instead of edges because they are antichains, and the violet w column is neither: it skips a level (w1 ≤ w3 but w1 ⊬ w2).
